# 1. Vision

## 1.1 Background

Telecommunication operators generate enormous amounts of data every day, including customer information, billing records, network quality metrics, customer service interactions, marketing campaigns, and user behavior.

Although most operators already own Business Intelligence (BI) systems, these platforms primarily answer descriptive questions:

- What happened?
- How many users churned?
- Which package generated the highest revenue?
- What is the current ARPU?

Traditional dashboards provide visibility into historical metrics but rarely explain **why changes occurred**, **what business impact they have**, or **what actions should be taken next**.

As telecom markets become increasingly competitive, operators require systems that move beyond visualization toward intelligent decision support.

InsightFlow is designed to fill this gap.

------

## 1.2 Vision Statement

> **Transform telecom data into evidence-driven operational decisions through AI-powered analytics, machine learning, and intelligent reasoning.**

InsightFlow is not another dashboard.

It is an AI-native Decision Intelligence Platform capable of converting large-scale operational data into actionable business recommendations supported by transparent evidence.

------

## 1.3 Product Mission

Enable telecom operators to:

- Understand customer behavior.
- Predict future risks.
- Discover hidden business opportunities.
- Recommend operational strategies.
- Automatically generate executive-level analytical reports.
- Improve business decisions using explainable AI.

------

## 1.4 Product Philosophy

InsightFlow follows four design principles.

### Evidence First

Every conclusion must be supported by verifiable evidence.

The system never generates unsupported recommendations.

Each finding must reference:

- Metrics
- Statistical evidence
- SQL results
- ML outputs
- Feature importance

------

### AI Assists Humans

AI provides analysis and recommendations.

Final business decisions remain under human control.

The system is an operational copilot rather than an autonomous decision maker.

------

### Explainability Over Black Box

Predictions without explanations are not acceptable.

Every prediction should answer:

- Why?
- How confident?
- Which factors contributed?
- What evidence supports the conclusion?

------

### Decision Instead of Dashboard

Visualization is only a communication layer.

The real value lies in:

Data

↓

Insight

↓

Evidence

↓

Recommendation

↓

Decision

# Part III — Data Platform Architecture

Version 1.0

# 1. Overview

## 1.1 Purpose

The Data Platform is the foundation of InsightFlow.

Its responsibilities are:

- Collect operational data from multiple telecom systems.
- Validate and clean raw datasets.
- Build an analytics-ready warehouse.
- Generate reusable machine learning features.
- Provide consistent datasets for analytics, AI, and reporting.

Every downstream module—including KPI Analytics, Customer 360, Churn Prediction, Recommendation Engine, and AI Copilot—must consume data exclusively through this platform.

------

## 1.2 Design Principles

### Single Source of Truth

Every business metric must originate from a single authoritative source.

No duplicated business logic is allowed across services.

------

### Immutable Raw Data

Raw datasets are append-only.

No downstream process may modify raw records.

------

### Reproducible Features

Feature generation must be deterministic.

The same input always produces the same feature values.

------

### Layered Architecture

Every data transformation belongs to one specific layer.

Cross-layer access is prohibited.

------

### Versioned Data

Schemas and feature definitions are version-controlled.

Every model can reproduce the exact dataset used for training.

------

# 1. Overview

## 1.1 Vision

The AI Copilot is the reasoning engine of InsightFlow.

It transforms business questions into structured analytical workflows by orchestrating multiple specialized AI agents.

Unlike traditional chatbots, the AI Copilot never answers questions directly.

Instead, it:

- Understands business intent.
- Plans analytical tasks.
- Retrieves structured evidence.
- Executes analytical workflows.
- Validates conclusions.
- Generates explainable reports.

Every response must be supported by verifiable data.

The AI Copilot follows four principles.

### Evidence before Language

The LLM never invents conclusions.

Every statement must reference retrieved evidence.

### Structured Reasoning

Complex analytical questions are decomposed into multiple reasoning steps.

No single agent performs every task.

------

### Separation of Responsibilities

Planning, analytics, retrieval, reporting, and validation are independent services.

Agents communicate through structured objects instead of natural language.

------

### Human-in-the-loop

AI proposes.

Humans decide.

The platform never performs autonomous business operations.

- JWT (MVP)
- OAuth 2.0 (future)
- OpenID Connect (future)
- Enterprise SSO (future)

Authorization follows Role-Based Access Control (RBAC).

Core roles

| Role               | Permissions                               |
| ------------------ | ----------------------------------------- |
| Administrator      | Full platform administration              |
| Data Engineer      | Data ingestion, ETL, warehouse management |
| Data Scientist     | Feature engineering, model training       |
| Business Analyst   | Analytics, reports, AI Copilot            |
| Operations Manager | Customer insights, recommendations        |
| Viewer             | Read-only access                          |

Permissions are additive and centrally managed.

------

# 4. Resource Authorization

Access is evaluated for every request.

Protected resources include

- Datasets
- Reports
- AI conversations
- Models
- Feature Store
- Prompt Registry
- Workflow executions

Authorization checks occur on the server side only.

------

# 5. Data Classification

Every dataset is assigned a classification.

| Level        | Description                                    |
| ------------ | ---------------------------------------------- |
| Public       | Safe for unrestricted access                   |
| Internal     | Internal operational information               |
| Confidential | Business-sensitive information                 |
| Restricted   | Customer-identifiable or highly sensitive data |

Classification determines storage, encryption, masking, and retention policies.

------

# 6. Personally Identifiable Information (PII)

PII examples include

- Customer name
- Phone number
- National identification number
- Email address
- Billing address

Requirements

- Encrypt sensitive fields at rest.
- Mask PII in logs and reports.
- Limit access to authorized roles.
- Avoid exposing PII to LLMs unless explicitly required and approved.

------

# 7. Data Governance

Every dataset must define:

- Data owner
- Business definition
- Refresh frequency
- Quality SLA
- Source system
- Lineage
- Retention period

No dataset may exist without registered metadata.

------

# 8. Data Lineage & Provenance

Every analytical output records:

- Source datasets
- ETL pipeline version
- Feature version
- Model version (if applicable)
- Report version
- Workflow ID

This enables end-to-end reproducibility.

------

# 9. AI Governance

Every AI workflow must declare:

- Prompt version
- Model version
- Temperature and key generation parameters
- Retrieved evidence
- Agent workflow version
- Timestamp

AI responses without provenance are considered invalid for production use.

------

# 10. Prompt Governance

Prompts are treated as version-controlled assets.

Every prompt includes

- Prompt ID
- Version
- Owner
- Purpose
- Evaluation status
- Last review date

Prompt changes require review and regression evaluation before deployment.

------

# 11. Model Governance

Every production model must have:

- Business owner
- Technical owner
- Approval history
- Deployment history
- Performance review schedule
- Retirement criteria

Unregistered models may not serve production traffic.

------

# 12. Decision Traceability

Every recommendation or report must expose:

- Supporting metrics
- Evidence references
- Model version (if ML is involved)
- AI workflow identifier
- Confidence score
- Generation timestamp

Business users should be able to understand *why* a recommendation was made.

------

# 13. Audit Logging

Immutable audit logs capture events such as:

- Login
- Permission changes
- Dataset access
- Model deployment
- Prompt updates
- Report publication
- AI conversation execution
- Administrative actions

Audit logs are retained according to organizational policy and are not editable.

------

# 14. Encryption

Requirements

- Encrypt data in transit using TLS.
- Encrypt sensitive data at rest.
- Rotate encryption keys periodically.
- Store secrets outside application code.

Encryption keys must never be embedded in source repositories.

------

# 15. Secure Development

Security requirements

- Parameterized database queries
- Input validation
- Output encoding
- Dependency vulnerability scanning
- Static application security testing
- Regular dependency updates

Security issues are prioritized according to severity.

------

# 16. Compliance Principles

The platform should support organizational compliance requirements, including:

- Data minimization
- Purpose limitation
- Access accountability
- Auditability
- Configurable retention and deletion policies

Compliance requirements may be extended for specific deployment environments.

------

# 17. AI Safety

AI-generated outputs must:

- Distinguish facts from interpretations.
- Cite supporting evidence where available.
- Avoid fabricating business metrics.
- Surface uncertainty through confidence indicators.
- Defer to human review for high-impact operational decisions.

Human oversight remains mandatory for actions with significant business consequences.

------

# 18. Governance Review

Governance assets are reviewed periodically.

Review scope

- Access permissions
- Data quality
- Prompt library
- Models
- Feature definitions
- Audit logs

Review outcomes are documented and version-controlled.

------

# 19. Acceptance Criteria

The governance framework is complete when:

- RBAC is enforced consistently.
- Sensitive data is classified and protected.
- Data lineage and provenance are available.
- AI workflows are versioned and traceable.
- Prompt and model governance are implemented.
- Audit logging is immutable.
- Security controls are documented and tested.
- Compliance principles are reflected in operational processes.

# 2. Product Delivery Strategy

InsightFlow is delivered incrementally.

Each phase must produce a working, demonstrable system.

Development priorities are:

1. **Correctness** – ensure features work as intended.
2. **Evidence** – every analytical conclusion is traceable.
3. **Reliability** – stable execution under realistic workloads.
4. **Scalability** – architecture supports future growth.
5. **Optimization** – performance improvements occur only after correctness.

Premature optimization is prohibited.
